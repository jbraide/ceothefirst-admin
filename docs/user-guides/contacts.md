# Contacts — Customers & Suppliers

The Contacts section is your address book for everyone you do business with. You can keep track of your **customers** (people who buy from you) and **suppliers** (people you buy from).

---

## Why Use Contacts?

Linking transactions to contacts gives you powerful insights:

- **See who owes you money** — If a customer buys on credit, that debt is automatically linked to their contact profile.
- **See who you owe** — If you buy stock on credit from a supplier, that debt is also tracked against their profile.
- **View transaction history** — You can pull up any contact and instantly see every transaction you've had with them.
- **Know your balance** — Each contact shows a running balance: positive means they owe you, negative means you owe them.

---

## Adding a Contact

To add someone, you'll need:

- **Name** — The person's or business's name (required).
- **Phone Number** — Their phone number (optional, but helpful).
- **Email Address** — Their email (optional).
- **Address** — Their physical address (optional).
- **Type** — Are they a **Customer**, a **Supplier**, or **Both**?

You can always update these details later.

---

## Contact Types Explained

| Type | Use When |
|------|----------|
| **Customer** | This person buys from your business. |
| **Supplier** | You buy goods or services from this person. |
| **Both** | You both sell to them and buy from them. |

---

## Understanding the Balance

Every contact has a **net balance** calculated automatically:

- **Positive balance → They owe you.** This usually happens when a customer buys on credit and hasn't fully paid.
- **Negative balance → You owe them.** This happens when you buy from a supplier on credit and haven't fully paid.
- **Zero balance → All settled.** Nothing outstanding either way.

The balance updates in real time whenever a transaction involving that contact is recorded.

---

## Viewing Transaction History

When you open a contact's details, you can see their full transaction history — every sale, purchase, or repayment linked to them. This is useful when:

- A customer calls asking how much they still owe.
- You need to review your purchase history with a supplier.
- You want to verify past payments before a dispute arises.

---

## Deleting a Contact

You can delete a contact, but only if they have **no transaction history** with you. This protects your financial records — once a contact has been part of a transaction, their record is preserved for your ledger's integrity.

If you no longer do business with someone who has history, you can simply stop using their contact. Their past records remain for your reports.
